# Multiclass Brain-Computer Interface Classification by Riemannian Geometry

## Mechanism (from PubMed abstract)

According to PubMed, Barachant et al. introduce Riemannian-geometry
classification on the manifold of symmetric positive-definite (SPD)
covariance matrices for motor-imagery BCI. Spatial covariance matrices
are used as EEG descriptors and classified directly using the AIRM
distance and Riemannian mean, bypassing spatial filtering entirely.
Two methods: MDRM (minimum distance to Riemannian mean) matches CSP +
LDA baselines; TSLDA (tangent-space projection then LDA) improves
multiclass accuracy on BCI Competition IV dataset IIa from 65.1% to
70.2%.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches behavioral-decoding, ism-v1, cortex-of-anyone
because SPD-manifold classification of covariance descriptors is the
foundational engineering pattern NEUROSPINE's `manifold.py` inherits.
Provisional: informs G6 (mechanism: the AIRM is provably invariant
under affine transformations, so a decoder that respects it cannot be
fooled by trivial re-referencing); G7 (calibration via tangent-space
distance); G8 (external validity across BCI competition datasets);
G14 (manifold correctness: MDRM and TSLDA are the numerical
identity-check reference).
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 (per-subject SPD manifold
classification with published baselines) and A4 (intervention: the
tangent-space projection is the technical basis for pushforward
computation in `intervention.py`).
