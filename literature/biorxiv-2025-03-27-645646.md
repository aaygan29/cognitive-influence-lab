# Trial-Level Representational Similarity Analysis

## Mechanism (from abstract)

The authors identify a structural limitation in classic representational similarity analysis (cRSA): because cRSA compares two similarity matrices computed by aggregating across many items, it cannot weight or examine the contribution of individual trials, limiting its ability to assess subject-, stimulus-, and trial-level variance, all of which affect representation strength. They introduce trial-level RSA (tRSA), a multi-level-model framework that estimates representational strength per individual experimental trial rather than only in aggregate. They verify tRSA and cRSA agree on overall representation strength, then show via simulation that tRSA is more statistically sensitive to true effects and more theoretically appropriate, and demonstrate on real fMRI data that tRSA is more robust to several issues that affect cRSA, uncovering novel representational findings unreachable by cRSA alone.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches tribe-neuroprint, memoryprint, and decision-phenotype broadly because RSA-style analysis is a standard tool for comparing neural representational geometry to a model or to behavior, and any current or planned use of RSA in these projects likely uses the classic (cRSA) form this paper shows is limited; tRSA is a direct upgrade path.
Provisional: touches bio-toolkit as a reusable analysis-method upgrade that should be adopted toolkit-wide rather than re-derived per project.
Provisional: informs gate G2 (seed variance) because a trial-level, multi-level model naturally supports reporting variance across trials (and by extension across resampled subsets), closer to what G2 requires than a single aggregate cRSA statistic.
Provisional: informs gate G9 (measurement reliability) because trial-level representation-strength estimates are a finer-grained reliability signal (per-trial consistency) than the single number cRSA yields, useful for quantifying within-subject measurement stability.
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by giving a more statistically sensitive tool for detecting individual-scale representational effects that a coarser aggregate method (cRSA) would miss or underpower, directly relevant to A1's replicability bar at the single-subject level.
