# Complementary benefits of multivariate and hierarchical models for identifying individual differences in cognitive control

## Mechanism (from PubMed abstract)

According to PubMed, this paper attacks the reliability crisis in
individual-differences neuroimaging head on. The authors argue that the
usual solution to noisy trial-level fMRI (compute an average) throws
out the information most needed to individuate people. They combine
hierarchical Bayesian modeling of trial-level fMRI data with
multivariate pattern decoding of the same data. Applied to Stroop task
data from the Dual Mechanisms of Cognitive Control project over
several months per subject, the combined approach reaches near-maximal
test-retest reliability in prefrontal and parietal regions, whereas
traditional univariate contrasts do not. The result is not that the
signal is missing; it is that averaging destroys it. Multivariate
decoding suppresses cross-trial variability that hierarchical modeling
cannot on its own.
