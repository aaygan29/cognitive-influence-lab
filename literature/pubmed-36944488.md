# Decoding of Working Memory Contents in Auditory Cortex Is Not Distractor-Resistant

## Mechanism (from PubMed abstract)

According to PubMed, Deutsch et al. asked participants to memorize
the pitch of a target sound and compare it with a probe after a
13 s delay, under three conditions: no distraction, passive
listening to auditory distractors, or active processing of the
distractors. Under no distraction, pitch-specific working memory
information decodes from auditory cortex. Under both passive and
active distraction, decoding drops to chance in early auditory
cortex, paralleled by memory-performance decrements. Exploratory
analysis suggests inferior frontal and superior parietal cortex may
carry memory content under active distraction. Direct contrast to
prior visual-cortex WM findings that DID show distractor resistance:
the sensory-cortex maintenance story does not generalize.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches memoryprint, behavioral-decoding,
cortex-of-anyone because WM-contents decoding under naturalistic
interference is a MemoryDecoder G4 (specificity) and G5 (confound
control) requirement for any real-world decoding target.
Provisional: informs G4 (specificity ablation: distraction is the
natural G4 control for any WM decoder); G5 (confound control: the
"sensory cortex holds WM" claim confounds distraction absence);
G6 (mechanism: the site of durable WM storage under distraction
appears to shift from sensory to frontoparietal cortex, changing
the necessity claim for any sensory-cortex decoder); G-fMRI.1
(within-subject decoding).
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by giving a concrete G4
protocol design for MemoryDecoder: the distraction-vs-no-distraction
contrast.
