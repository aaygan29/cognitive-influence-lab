# Cortex Deviates from Criticality During Action and Deep Sleep: A Temporal Renormalization Group Approach

## Mechanism (from abstract)

The authors develop "temporal renormalization group" (tRG) theory, a formal import of statistical-mechanics renormalization-group methods, for analyzing proximity of neural systems to criticality using ordinary short time-series data, rather than the long recordings classical criticality analysis usually requires. They mathematically identify multiple distinct types of criticality (tRG fixed points) and build data-analytic methods to measure distance from each fixed point at behaviorally relevant timescales. Applied to mouse visual cortex spike recordings across brain states, the relaxed awake state is closest to criticality; both increased arousal (active awake) and decreased arousal (deep sleep) move cortical dynamics away from criticality in a time-resolved, quantifiable way.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches anesthesia-bridge most directly and is the strongest physics-anchor candidate found in this scan: tRG gives a formal, short-time-series-compatible method for measuring graded distance from criticality, exactly the kind of complexity-battery component (alongside LZc/sigma/VNE/Phi*) the Anesthesia Bridge project needs, and it is explicitly validated across a graded range of brain/arousal states (deep sleep to active), the same design as graded propofol anesthesia.
Provisional: touches tribe-neuroprint because "distance from criticality" is a state-dependent, time-resolved covariate that should be checked as a confound or feature for any decoder trained across different arousal/behavioral states.
Provisional: touches wiring-not-weights indirectly because criticality is a network-dynamics property that could in principle be decomposed into wiring-driven vs. weight/gain-driven contributions, a candidate axis for the wiring-vs-weights ablation ladder.
Provisional: informs gate G6 (mechanism and necessity) because tRG identifies specific fixed points (mechanistic, falsifiable predictions) and the paper tests distance from each across a manipulated variable (arousal state), giving both a mechanism and a graded intervention.
Provisional: informs gate G9 (measurement reliability) because tRG is explicitly built to work on short, behaviorally-relevant timescales rather than requiring long stationary recordings, directly relevant to whether NEUROSPINE's complexity metrics are reliable on the shorter windows real task data provides.
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by providing a time-resolved, individual-recording-compatible criticality metric, and aim A2 because it is validated at the population/group level (mouse visual cortex, replicated pattern across arousal states) with a quantified, graded relationship to state.
