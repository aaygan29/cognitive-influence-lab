# Reproducible brain-wide association studies require thousands of individuals

## Mechanism (from PubMed abstract)

According to PubMed, Marek et al. quantified brain-wide association
study (BWAS) effect sizes and reproducibility across three of the
largest available neuroimaging datasets (total N ~ 50,000). Result:
BWAS associations are much smaller than previously thought.
Typical-sample studies (median N ~ 25) are statistically
underpowered, produce inflated effect sizes, and fail to replicate.
As N grows into the thousands, replication rates improve and effect
size inflation decreases. Functional MRI produces more robust
associations than structural, cognitive tests more robust than
mental-health questionnaires, and multivariate methods more robust
than univariate. Contrast: non-BWAS approaches (lesions,
interventions, within-person designs) show larger effects at smaller
N; the sample-size demand is specific to across-person association
studies.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches every portfolio project that either fits a
BWAS-style association or compares across subjects: behavioral-
decoding, memoryprint, cortex-of-anyone, decision-phenotype,
tribe-neuroprint.
Provisional: informs G2 (seed-equivalent variance: across-subject
BWAS-style estimates need thousands of subjects for replicability);
G7 (calibration: confidence intervals on across-subject effects
must reflect the small true effect sizes); G8 (external validity:
transfer across subjects at small N is dominated by noise); G12
(analytic integrity: report the exact N and effect-size CI, not the
inflated point estimate).
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by drawing the boundary
between within-subject (where MEIcoder-style small-N is fine) and
across-subject association (where Marek's thousands are required).
Aim A2 (group scale) inherits Marek's sample-size demand.
