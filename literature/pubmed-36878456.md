# A multivariate brain signature for reward

## Mechanism (from PubMed abstract)

According to PubMed, Speer et al. trained a Brain Reward Signature
(BRS) on the Monetary Incentive Delay task (n=39) to predict the
signed magnitude of monetary rewards. They achieved 92 percent
decoding accuracy for rewards versus losses. Generalization to a
different sample of the same task (n=12) also hit 92 percent; to a
large gambling task (n=1084) hit 73 percent. Preliminary specificity:
BRS estimates differ significantly between rewarding and negative
feedback but not between conditions differing in disgust rather than
reward on a novel Disgust-Delay Task (n=39). Passive viewing of
positive and negative facial expressions loaded positively on the
signature, consistent with a broader "salience or morbid curiosity"
interpretation the authors flag. The signature is distributed across
regions; regional-averaged approaches yield smaller effects.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches nacc-anticipation, decision-phenotype,
behavioral-decoding, warden because it is a validated, distributed,
public reward-decoder trained on MID and tested on gambling.
Provisional: informs G6 (mechanism / necessity, via the disgust
control), G7 (calibration), G8 (external validity across two samples
and two tasks), G-fMRI.2 (implied per-subject concordance from the
92 percent generalization), G-fMRI.3 (group significance).
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 (as the reference
RewardDecoder external anchor) and A2 (via the demonstrated
task-level generalization from MID to gambling).
