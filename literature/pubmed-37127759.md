# Semantic reconstruction of continuous language from non-invasive brain recordings

## Mechanism (from PubMed abstract)

According to PubMed, Tang, LeBel, Jain, and Huth introduce a
non-invasive language decoder that reconstructs continuous language
from cortical semantic representations recorded via fMRI. Given
novel brain recordings, the decoder generates intelligible word
sequences that recover the meaning of perceived speech, imagined
speech, and even silent videos. A single decoder transfers across
these three input types. Continuous language can be separately
decoded from multiple cortical regions, not one specialized region.
The authors deliberately test mental-privacy questions: subject
cooperation is required both to train and to apply the decoder,
which they present as an ethical safety feature.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches tribe-neuroprint, memoryprint,
behavioral-decoding, cortex-of-anyone because a working non-invasive
continuous-language decoder is the closest published precedent for
NEUROSPINE's PerceptionDecoder + MemoryDecoder cross-input transfer
target (perceived + imagined + silent).
Provisional: informs G1 (provenance and subject-cooperation
guarantee); G6 (mechanism: multiple regions decode independently);
G8 (external validity: three input modalities); G11 (ethics: mental
privacy requires cooperation-gated training and inference);
G-fMRI.1 and G-fMRI.2 (per-subject decoders that generalize to new
speech).
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by giving a published
recipe for continuous-perception decoding on per-subject fMRI; A3
by demonstrating that the study should declare "requires subject
cooperation" as a first-class boundary condition.
