# Simultaneous, cortex-wide dynamics of up to 1 million neurons reveal unbounded scaling of dimensionality with neuron number

## Mechanism (from PubMed abstract)

According to PubMed, Manley et al. image up to 1 million mouse
cortical neurons simultaneously at cellular resolution using light
beads microscopy, then apply dimensionality reduction as populations
grow. Result: dimensionality of neural activity scales unboundedly
with neuron number. Half of the variance is contained in 16
behavior-correlated dimensions, but higher dimensions correspond to
increasing numbers of neuronal ensembles without immediate
behavioral or sensory correlates. The activity patterns underlying
these higher dimensions are fine-grained and cortex-wide.
Implication: low-dim latent claims from small-N recordings miss the
substrate of neural computation; capturing it requires large-scale
cellular-resolution recording.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches wiring-not-weights (higher-dim, cortex-wide
ensembles are candidate carriers of the "identity" signal that
low-dim latents miss), cortex-of-anyone, memoryprint,
tribe-neuroprint, behavioral-decoding (all of these must confront
the possibility that their low-dim projections discard the substrate
of computation).
Provisional: informs G8 (external validity: results derived from
small-N or low-dim recordings do not automatically transfer to the
large-N regime); G9 (measurement reliability: the "fine-grained,
cortex-wide" higher dimensions imply reliability targets specific to
the recording scale); G12 (analytic integrity: dimensionality claims
must state the neuron count at which they were derived).
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by warning that per-subject
replicability tested at one recording scale may not survive at
another; A3 (Cognitive Dark Matter): the higher unbounded dimensions
are candidate substrates for the domains NEUROSPINE explicitly does
not attempt to predict.
