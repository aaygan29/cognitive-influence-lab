# Analog Cognition and Consciousness

## Mechanism (from PubMed abstract)

According to PubMed, Miller, Brincat, and Roy argue that cognition
and consciousness arise from bidirectional interactions between
neuronal spiking and rhythmic electric field activity (brain waves).
Goal-directed behavior requires top-down control to coordinate large
neural populations into low-dimensional, task-oriented dynamics.
Brain waves are well suited for this role, exerting mesoscale
influence over neural excitability. They can also support analog
computation, shaping activity patterns according to underlying
computational principles. Brain waves flexibly route and organize
neural signals, enabling multifunctional neurons to assume
context-dependent roles, a hallmark of cognition. In this view,
goal-directed thought, action, and unified consciousness emerge from
cortex-wide wave dynamics that both reflect and transform spiking
into coherent brain states.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches ism-v1 (the closed-loop self-model has wave
dynamics as a candidate readout for top-down control state);
cortex-of-anyone (individuation targets should include per-subject
wave-dynamic fingerprints, not only per-timepoint activation
patterns); wiring-not-weights (wave dynamics are a "beyond
connectome" identity signal in the same class Aayush's memory names
as the identity substrate); tribe-neuroprint, memoryprint,
behavioral-decoding (their spiking / fMRI reads should be
co-conditioned on mesoscale wave state); mats-jlens (analog wave
computation is a candidate mechanism for the multi-hop routing the
J-space work interrogates).
Provisional: informs G6 (mechanism / necessity: wave-mediated
routing is the falsifiable mechanism for multifunctional-neuron
context switching); G7 (calibration should be conditioned on wave
state, since the same spiking read means different things under
different mesoscale states); G-fMRI.1/2/3 (the reliability triad
should test whether decoders are stable across wave-state slices,
not only overall).
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by giving a principled
mechanistic reason why per-subject replicability is achievable
(subjects live at consistent wave-state manifolds); A2 (cross-
subject transfer should test whether wave-manifold alignment
predicts transfer cost); A3 (unified-consciousness framing sharpens
the Cognitive Dark Matter declaration boundary).
