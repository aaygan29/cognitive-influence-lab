# Direction of Motion Decoding in Mouse V1: Neuron Predictive Power Relates to Functional Connectivity Organization

## Mechanism (from abstract)

Using mesoscopic two-photon calcium imaging of hundreds of neurons in mouse V1, the authors identify neurons with high predictive power (HPP) for stimulus direction via mutual information, under 16 directions of motion. HPP neurons show elevated firing rates during stimulus presentation and denser, stronger, distance-independent functional connectivity among themselves during stimulation, forming a distributed but coordinated subnetwork. Critically, this HPP-specific connectivity structure is state-dependent: during resting state (no stimulus), HPP neurons show lower event rates and their functional connectivity is statistically indistinguishable from other visually responsive neurons, meaning the decodable subnetwork's structure is not a fixed, always-on property but emerges with task engagement. Tuning properties of HPP neurons were unexpectedly heterogeneous: some sharply tuned, others weakly tuned but still highly informative.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches tribe-neuroprint and decision-phenotype because the paper shows that a decodable subnetwork's connectivity signature is state-dependent (present under stimulus, absent at rest), a direct confound for any decoder that assumes functional connectivity is a stable, state-independent feature.
Provisional: informs gate G4 (specificity ablation) because the resting-state condition functions as a built-in matched control: the same neurons, no stimulus, and the HPP-specific connectivity signature disappears, precisely the kind of control G4 requires to show a decodable effect is not an artifact of neuron identity alone.
Provisional: informs gate G5 (confound control) because "behavioral/arousal state at time of recording" is shown here to be a first-order confound for functional-connectivity-based decoding, which NEUROSPINE's PerceptionDecoder and DecisionDecoder pipelines must enumerate and control for explicitly.
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by demonstrating, in a well-controlled animal model, that individual-scale, information-based selection of a small decodable neural subnetwork is possible and mechanistically interpretable (tuning heterogeneity plus connectivity), a template for what an individual-scale replicable decoding claim should look like methodologically, even though the species and modality differ from NEUROSPINE's target.
