# Embedding of low-dimensional sensory dynamics in recurrent networks: implications for the geometry of neural representation

## Mechanism (from PubMed abstract)

According to PubMed, O'Reilly-Shah and Selvitella prove, via
generalized synchronization plus delay-embedding theory, that
contracting recurrent networks generically develop smooth internal
manifolds that embed the sensory dynamics driving them. The
dimensional requirement depends only on the intrinsic dimension of
the effective sensory manifold, not the world's complexity, and is
bounded by Whitney and Takens embedding theorems (e.g. hidden
dimension 3 for a circle, 5 for a two-frequency torus). They prove
a prediction-separation result: if the circuit predicts future
sensory inputs with small error, then states with different futures
must be separated in neural state space up to a resolution set by
the prediction error. Numerical experiments recover ring- and
torus-shaped hidden manifolds with expected topology; training
often pushes networks beyond strict contraction, yet manifold
recovery persists.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches wiring-not-weights (identity may live in the
embedding geometry of the subject's internal manifold, not the
raw connectome), cortex-of-anyone (per-subject embedding geometry
is a candidate individuation invariant), memoryprint,
behavioral-decoding (the prediction-separation result gives a
principled bound on decoder resolution given achieved prediction
error).
Provisional: informs G6 (mechanism / necessity: the embedding
theorems tell us how much internal dimension is needed for a given
prediction task, so under-dimensioned decoders can be diagnosed
mathematically); G8 (external validity: same sensory dynamics
should give the same topology across subjects, testable); G12
(analytic integrity: dimensionality-of-latent claims should now be
tied to the intrinsic dimension of the driving sensory manifold,
not to arbitrary PCA thresholds).
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by giving a math-first
theory of why per-subject decoders can be low-dim and stable; A2
by giving a topological-alignment target for cross-subject
transfer; A3 by making the "unmeasured" declaration formal (any
Cognitive Dark Matter domain not driving a stable embedding is
correctly not predicted).
