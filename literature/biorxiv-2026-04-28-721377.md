# Causal Discovery of Synchronous Neural Oscillations based on Jacobian-informed VAR-LiNGAM

## Mechanism (from abstract)

The authors address a known weakness of standard "Granger causality"-style effective-connectivity methods in neuroscience: they cannot reliably identify causal hierarchy in modular brain networks because of spurious correlations and, under Gaussian observational noise in a linear system, indistinguishability of causal direction. They propose "j-VAR-LiNGAM," which incorporates a Jacobian matrix estimated from a phase-coupled oscillator model into the linear non-Gaussian acyclic model (LiNGAM) framework for vector-autoregressive causal discovery on synchronous neural oscillations. Validated on synthetic data (ground-truth causal ordering known) and on empirical mouse and human neural oscillatory recordings, the method recovers causally hierarchical structures that align with established neurophysiological interpretation.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches tribe-neuroprint and decision-phenotype because most current connectivity-based decoders in the instrument likely rely on correlational or Granger-style effective connectivity, which this paper explicitly shows can misidentify causal direction under Gaussian noise; j-VAR-LiNGAM is a candidate replacement or cross-check method.
Provisional: touches nacc-anticipation because a validated causal-discovery method for oscillatory neural data is directly applicable to disentangling anticipatory (reward-prediction) signal flow from confounding feedback loops in the NAcc task's time-series analysis.
Provisional: informs gate G5 (confound control) because the paper's central point, that standard Granger-causality methods are confounded by spurious correlation and noise-Gaussianity assumptions, is itself a confound NEUROSPINE must check for in any causal or directional claim about neural signal flow.
Provisional: informs gate G6 (mechanism and necessity) because the method is validated against known synthetic ground truth (a necessity/sufficiency check on the causal-discovery algorithm itself) before being applied to real data, modeling the standard G6 needs at the level of an analysis method rather than a scientific claim.
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by offering a more rigorous causal-discovery tool for individual-scale time-series data, potentially strengthening any claim in the instrument that asserts a causal (not merely correlational) direction of neural signal flow.
