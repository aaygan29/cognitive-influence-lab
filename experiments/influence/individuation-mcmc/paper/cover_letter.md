To the Editors of Computational Brain & Behavior

I am submitting the manuscript "Individuating the decision process from behavior: recoverability, test-retest reliability, and the limits of inferring susceptibility to covert steering" for consideration as an Original Research article.

The paper asks a simple question with a careful answer: how far does behavior, choices and reaction times alone, support inferences about an individual, from recovering their decision parameters, to re-identifying them across sessions, to estimating how cheaply their decisions could be covertly steered. I fit a hierarchical Bayesian drift-diffusion model with a reinforcement-learning rule, and I treat each claim as a separate empirical gate rather than assuming that a model which predicts choices also predicts manipulability.

The central result is positive and tested on real data: on a test-retest reversal-learning dataset, a decision-state fingerprint fit to a participant in one session predicts that same participant in a later session better than another participant's fingerprint in 115 of 150 cases. The paper is equally explicit about what behavior does not support. A susceptibility estimate taken from the reduced model fails to recover the value in the full generative circuit, which recovers only when susceptibility is computed in the circuit itself by simulation-based inference, and susceptibility is never validated on a real person. I believe this honest separation, and the mix of hierarchical Bayesian modeling, simulation-based inference, and a real-data individuation test, fits the methodological and cognitive-modeling scope of Computational Brain & Behavior.

The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been published before. I am the sole author and have approved the submission. I have no competing interests to declare. The human data are from a published, openly licensed dataset and are analyzed only in aggregate; code and aggregate results are available in a public repository.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
