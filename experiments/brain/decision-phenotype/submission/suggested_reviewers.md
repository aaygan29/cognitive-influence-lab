# Suggested reviewers / experts to approach for feedback

Two uses: (a) names to suggest as reviewers on the NBDT form, and (b) people to send the arXiv
preprint to for a "second pair of eyes" before or during review. Do not suggest anyone with a conflict
(recent collaborators, advisors). Pick a few, not all.

## Neuroforecasting / affective decision neuroscience (closest fit)
- **Brian Knutson** (Stanford) — the AIM framework and neuroforecasting; the most on-topic reader.
- **Alexander Genevsky** (Erasmus/Rotterdam) — the "brain beats behavior" out-of-sample line.
- **Gabriela Samanez-Larkin** (Duke) — AIM and affect; would speak to the affective/integrative split.
- **Mirre Stallen** — neuroforecasting of markets.

## Value / decision fMRI (mixed-gambles paradigm)
- **Russell Poldrack** (Stanford) — NARPS and the Tom-2007 mixed-gambles task; ideal for the fMRI arm.
- **Rotem Botvinik-Nezer** — led NARPS; expert on analysis variability, which our honesty stance speaks to.

## Computational modeling of behavior (methods)
- **Quentin Huys** (UCL) — computational psychiatry, generative models, identifiability (our honesty
  theme). Good fit for the estimator + identifiability result.
- A reviewer versed in **conformal prediction / distribution-free UQ** (e.g., from the
  Angelopoulos-Bates line) for the honesty-layer guarantees.

## For direct feedback (preprint outreach, not necessarily reviewers)
- Faculty in decision neuroscience / computational cognition at your target programs. Send the arXiv
  link with the plain-language summary and one specific ask ("does the triangulation / transitivity
  argument hold up?" is the sharpest thing to have checked).

Note: NBDT is an arXiv-overlay journal, so posting the preprint IS the first submission step and
doubles as the vehicle for getting outside eyes on it.
