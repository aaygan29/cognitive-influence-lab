# Linking Human Brain Functional Connectivity to Underlying Neurotransmission

## Mechanism (from abstract)

The authors adopt a topological framework (termed NEOFC, linking neurobiology to the functional connectome) to quantify the association between regional neurotransmitter receptor/transporter distributions and functional connectivity from resting-state fMRI and MEG, across six healthy adult cohorts (n=19-112). They find that low-frequency rsfMRI synchronization is predominantly modulated by decreased regional receptor/transporter availability, a pattern present in every single subject, replicated across all six cohorts, and mirrored (in the opposite direction: increases with availability) in MEG high-frequency synchronization. The most prominent finding is noradrenergic modulation of connectivity in a sensorimotor-posterior-insular network, consistently detected per-subject and linked to autonomic arousal; the pattern is further shown to be sensitive to pharmacological and clinical manipulation (altered in early psychosis, aligned with symptomatology).

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches tribe-neuroprint because per-subject, cross-cohort replicated neurotransmitter-connectivity coupling is a strong example of the individual-plus-group replicability standard NEUROSPINE's own connectivity-based claims must meet.
Provisional: touches affectprint because noradrenergic modulation linked to autonomic arousal is directly relevant to AffectDecoder's need for a neurochemically grounded arousal/affect signal, not just a behavioral proxy.
Provisional: touches anesthesia-bridge because a neurotransmitter-receptor-availability account of connectivity synchronization is a mechanistic bridge to how anesthetic agents (acting on specific receptor systems) should be predicted to alter the connectivity/complexity battery (LZc/sigma/VNE/Phi*).
Provisional: informs gate G8 (external validity) because the pattern replicates across six independent cohorts, one of the strongest external-validity demonstrations found in this scan.
Provisional: informs gates G-fMRI.1 and G-fMRI.2 because the finding is explicitly reported as present in every single subject (per-participant, satisfying G-fMRI.1's spirit) and as consistent in direction across the group (supporting a sign-concordance test per G-fMRI.2), though the paper does not report a formal binomial sign-concordance statistic and this must be re-derived from their per-subject data if available.
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A2 by being one of the cleanest group-scale generalization results in this scan: single-subject-level presence, six-cohort replication, and quantified sensitivity to pharmacological/clinical perturbation, directly the shape A2's "quantified degradation" evidence should take.
