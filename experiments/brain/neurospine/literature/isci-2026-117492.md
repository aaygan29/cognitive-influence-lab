# Neural subspace reorganization reflects value-based decision-making

## Mechanism (from abstract)

Two non-human primates hold two sequentially presented option-value
pairs in working memory before choosing between them. Lateral
prefrontal cortex population activity (316 and 422 neurons across the
two animals) represents the pre-decision options in near-orthogonal
subspaces organized by presentation ORDER (first-presented vs.
second-presented), from an overlapping, mixed-selective population
rather than separate order-coded subpopulations. After the decision
(at the second reward cue), the representation reorganizes: chosen and
unchosen options rotate into near-orthogonal CHOICE-coded subspaces,
the chosen-option representation expands (between-class variance across
chosen conditions increases, within-class variance is unchanged), and
chosen options from both presentation orders align into a common
subspace that supports a single decoder trained on one order
generalizing to the other. The choice axis is geometrically orthogonal
to the location/order subspaces. The value signal itself precedes the
reorganization in time. Code released at github.com/hdl1115/wmUpdate.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches neurospine's trajectory and propagation layers
(`hmm.py`, `dynamics.py`, `manifold.py`) because it grounds the
memory-to-decision handoff as a GEOMETRIC transform on a neural
subspace (order-coded to choice-coded, with a measurable alignment and
expansion signature) rather than as a scalar value comparison. This is a
direct external anchor for representing NEUROSPINE's
memory-state-to-decision transition as a change in the manifold
embedding's coordinate frame, not only as a change in a scalar
DDM-style drift/threshold parameter.
Provisional: informs gate G6 (mechanism/necessity) by supplying a
specific, falsifiable geometric signature that a NEUROSPINE
memory-to-decision handoff should reproduce if it is claiming to model
the same computation: post-decision alignment of chosen-condition
representations across two different presentation orders into a common,
cross-order-decodable subspace, with expansion (between-class variance
up) but not within-class-variance change. A NEUROSPINE claim that its
manifold trajectory implements a decision handoff should be checked
against this alignment-and-expansion pattern before being called
mechanistically grounded.
Provisional: informs gate G12 (dimensionality claims paired with
recording scale) because the reported subspaces are defined and
validated at a specific recording scale (300-450 unit LPFC populations
in two animals) with a small, discrete option set; any claim that
NEUROSPINE's lower-dimensional AIRM tangent embedding recovers an
equivalent geometric reorganization needs to state the scale mismatch
explicitly.
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 narrowly as a mechanistic
external constraint (not as data NEUROSPINE currently fits): the value
signal preceding the geometric reorganization, and the reorganization
being population-level and multiplexed across an overlapping neuron
population rather than in dedicated subpopulations, are properties any
individual-scale decision-decoder claim should be consistent with.

## Limitations (from the paper, carried forward)

Discrete, small option-value set; correlational (no causal
manipulation of the subspace geometry); two animals.
