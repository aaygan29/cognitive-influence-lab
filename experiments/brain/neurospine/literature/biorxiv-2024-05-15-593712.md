# Synaptic Pruning Facilitates Online Bayesian Model Selection

## Mechanism (from abstract)

The authors present "Bayesian synaptic model pruning" (BSyMP), a scheme that extends canonical neural networks so that synaptic connectivity parameters switch between present (ON) and absent (OFF) states in a way that is formally equivalent to online Bayesian model selection, i.e. the network prunes uninformative connections as a byproduct of ordinary learning dynamics. Mathematical analysis shows connectivity parameters for uninformative connections provably converge to zero, giving reliable and efficient model reduction, particularly when the true environmental model has sparse likelihood and transition structure. Causal-inference and rule-learning simulations show BSyMP achieves model reduction more efficiently than conventional Bayesian model reduction, and the authors propose synaptic pruning as a plausible neuronal substrate for structure learning and generalizability in the brain, i.e. this is a world-model / generative-model structure-learning mechanism grounded in a biologically motivated wiring process.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches decision-phenotype because Bayesian model selection via pruning is directly relevant to how the AIM-DDM's honesty/abstention layer could represent structural uncertainty about which decision-relevant variables actually matter, converging toward a minimal sufficient model.
Provisional: touches warden because a formal, mathematically grounded model-selection mechanism (with provable convergence for uninformative connections) is a candidate building block for calibrated abstention (H2): the network's own pruning dynamics could be read out as a confidence/relevance signal.
Provisional: touches cortex-of-anyone because pruning-as-model-selection is a wiring-level (not purely weight-magnitude) account of how a personal digital brain model should discover which of its own connections are behaviorally load-bearing versus noise, relevant to enrollment efficiency.
Provisional: informs gate G6 (mechanism and necessity) because the paper supplies a provable, falsifiable mathematical mechanism (parameters for uninformative connections converge to zero) tested against conventional Bayesian model reduction as a baseline comparison, though validated only in simulation, not against real neural pruning data.
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by proposing a concrete, individual-model structure-learning mechanism (which connections matter for this specific model/subject) that could inform how SubjectAdapter decides which features are subject-specific versus shared.
