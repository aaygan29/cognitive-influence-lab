# Toward Robust Neuroanatomical Normative Models: Influence of Sample Size and Covariates Distributions

## Mechanism (from abstract)

The authors systematically test how normative brain models (which characterize an individual's deviation from a healthy-control reference distribution) depend on the size and demographic composition of the reference cohort. Using OASIS-3 (n=1032) as a discovery dataset, they train models on healthy-control subsamples from 5 to 600 individuals, deliberately biasing age and sex distributions to simulate realistic reference-population skew, and separately test transfer learning by pretraining on UK Biobank (n=42,747) before adapting to the clinical dataset. Findings, replicated in an external validation sample (AIBL, n=463): performance improves with sample size, but demographic alignment (especially age) of the reference cohort is essential for reliable deviation estimates; directly trained models stabilize around 200 healthy controls, while pretrained-then-adapted models reach comparable performance with as few as 50 individuals.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches tribe-neuroprint and cortex-of-anyone because "how many reference subjects are needed for a reliable individual-level deviation score" is exactly the sample-size question any per-subject decoder or enrollment pipeline (SubjectAdapter) must answer, and this paper gives a concrete number (about 50 with pretraining, about 200 without) plus a demographic-matching requirement.
Provisional: touches bio-toolkit as a reusable normative-modeling method and sample-size heuristic applicable across projects that compare an individual to a reference population.
Provisional: informs gate G2 (seed variance) because the sample-size sweep (5 to 600) is itself a variance-characterization exercise NEUROSPINE's own decoders should replicate before trusting small-sample individual-level claims.
Provisional: informs gate G8 (external validity) because the result is independently replicated in a third dataset (AIBL) beyond the discovery and pretraining cohorts, meeting the external-validity bar directly.
Provisional: informs gate G9 (measurement reliability) because demographic mismatch between reference and target degrades deviation-score reliability, a confound any individual-level decoder must check and report.
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 by giving a concrete minimum-sample-size and demographic-matching requirement for reliable individual-scale prediction, and supports A2 by showing how transfer/pretraining changes the required reference-cohort size, i.e. quantified degradation/benefit from group-level pretraining.
