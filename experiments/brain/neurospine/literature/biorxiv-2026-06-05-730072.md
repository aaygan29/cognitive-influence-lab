# The role of superior colliculus in a logistic decision

## Mechanism (from abstract)

Mice perform an audiovisual left/right choice task while the superior
colliculus (SC) is optogenetically inactivated, unilaterally or
bilaterally, on individual trials. Unilateral SC inactivation reduces
the fraction of choices contralateral to the inactivated side without
changing sensory sensitivity (the slope of the psychometric function to
stimulus strength is unchanged; only the intercept/offset shifts).
Bilateral inactivation of both SC hemispheres produces an approximately
additive combination of the two unilateral effects, and a "balanced"
bilateral inactivation (matched strength on both sides) restores left/
right choice balance rather than abolishing choice altogether. The
authors fit choice behavior with a logistic model in which the decision
is a logistic function of a weighted sum of terms, and each SC
hemisphere contributes an independent, additive term that promotes the
contralateral action, separable from the sensory evidence term that
sets the slope.

## Provisional relevance

Provisional: touches neurospine's propagation/circuit layer
(`propagation.py`, `circuit.py`, `signed_dynamics.py`) because it
grounds a specific, testable decomposition of a region's contribution
to a downstream choice: a signed ADDITIVE bias on the action logit,
separate from a MULTIPLICATIVE effect on sensitivity to the evidence.
NEUROSPINE's directed-circuit and signed-linear-dynamics modules already
represent regions as sources of signed drive into downstream nodes
(ADR-014, ADR-015); this paper is an anchor for treating a region's
knockout effect on a binary/left-right decision specifically as a bias
shift rather than a slope shift, and for the additivity of two such
bias terms under joint (bilateral-style) inactivation.
Provisional: informs gate G6 (mechanism/necessity) because the paper's
own design is exactly a necessity test (in-vivo optogenetic lesion) with
a falsifiable, quantitative prediction (bias changes, sensitivity does
not; two lesions combine additively); a NEUROSPINE in-silico lesion of a
propagation-model region feeding a decision node should be checked
against the same signature (bias vs. sensitivity dissociation,
additivity across two knocked-out sources) before any necessity claim is
made from simulation alone.
Provisional: informs gate G8 (external validity) as a scope caveat: the
finding is mouse midbrain (SC), a two-alternative forced-choice
audiovisual task, and a subcortical structure NEUROSPINE's cortical
Schaefer parcellation does not include by default; any transfer of the
"additive signed bias, separable from sensitivity" pattern to a human
cortical decision circuit is an assumption to flag, not an established
cross-species result.
Provisional: supports NEUROSPINE aim A1 (individual-scale replicable
thought prediction) narrowly, by supplying an external, mechanistic
constraint on what a `DecisionDecoder`-style read-out of a region's
causal role should look like (additive logit bias with a knockout test),
rather than by supplying data NEUROSPINE can currently fit.
